A quarry, a prison, and the water that took both back

Rummu began as a limestone quarry worked by inmates of the neighbouring Murru prison. When the quarry closed and the pumps stopped, groundwater did what groundwater does — and buildings, machinery and quarry walls slipped below the surface of a new lake at the foot of a white spoil hill.

For freedivers, that history is the whole point. This is not a swimming beach with a view; it is a flooded industrial landscape where a single breath takes you along walls and past structures that were never meant to be underwater.

Rummu rewards exactly the qualities freediving training builds: calm, controlled movement, good buddy habits and respect for a site that changes with weather and season.

That is why every Rummu dive we guide starts on dry land — with an honest conversation about your experience and the day's conditions.

Who a guided Rummu dive is for

A guided fundive at Rummu is not a beginner course. It is for people who already freedive and want to experience an unusual site with local guidance. Before confirming a dive, we ask about:

  • your freediving level — a certification, or experience we can verify with you;
  • when you last dived, and how regularly you are in the water;
  • the depth you are genuinely comfortable at — not your personal best;
  • the equipment you own, especially cold-water exposure protection;
  • your group size and preferred dates.

New to freediving? Take the Start Here route instead — a first course is a far better first meeting with cold water than a fundive.

Safety and the local guide come before the view

Every dive runs on a buddy system with a guide who dives Rummu across the seasons. Briefings cover the site, the plan, signals and limits before anyone enters the water. Depth comes after calm, technique and a buddy — in that order.

The site is conditional by nature: wind, water temperature, visibility, ice and access can all change between booking and dive day.The final go/no-go decision always belongs to the local guide.A postponed dive is a normal outcome here, not a failure of planning.

Getting there and what to expect on land

Rummu lies in western Harju county, roughly an hour's drive from Tallinn. Most visitors rent a car or arrange transport when planning their trip — tell us in the suitability check if you need help thinking that through.

Facilities at the site are basic, and access arrangements can change; we confirm the current meeting point and practical details with every group before dive day.

Rummu questions, answered honestly

Can beginners dive Rummu?

A guided Rummu fundive is not a beginner course. If freediving is new to you, start with a beginner course first — the Start Here page shows the way. Rummu will still be here when you are ready.

Is the dive guaranteed to happen on my chosen date?

No open-water dive here is ever guaranteed. Weather, water temperature, visibility, ice and site access all change, and the local guide makes the final go/no-go decision. We always discuss realistic dates and backup options with you.

What will I actually see underwater?

Quarry walls, flooded structures and equipment left behind when the pumps stopped — in northern water whose character shifts with the season. What is safely reachable on a given day depends on conditions and on your experience.

Do I need my own equipment?

Bring your own if you have it — cold-water exposure protection matters here. Tell us what you are missing in the suitability check and we will discuss options before your trip.

How do I actually book?

Through the suitability check on our service site — it asks about your experience, comfort depth and dates, and the team replies personally: check dates and suitability.

Trained, curious and thinking about dates?

Tell the team your level, comfort depth and travel window — you will get a personal, honest answer about whether and when Rummu fits.
